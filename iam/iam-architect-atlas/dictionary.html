Reading Guide
How to use this dictionary effectively
Strategies, tools, scenarios, and shortcuts — read this once, use the dictionary for years
▼
🏗️ Before an architecture review
  • Filter to the relevant domain (e.g. Protocols, Cloud Identity)
  • Open the Mind Map to orient yourself on concept relationships
  • Pin the 5–8 terms most likely to come up using the 📌 button
  • Use Expand All to scan all definitions in one pass
  • Check Confusion Radar for pairs your team typically conflates
⚡ During a design discussion
  • Press / to instantly jump to search — no scrolling
  • Type the term name — results filter as you type
  • Click the term row to expand the full architectural definition
  • Share the ⚠️ Often confused with note to resolve terminology disagreements
  • Click the module tag to pull up the deep-dive module in a new tab
📚 Studying a new domain
  • Click the domain pill to isolate that domain completely
  • Start with the Mind Map — understand the concept landscape before the terms
  • Use Expand All to read through all definitions sequentially
  • Take the Test Yourself quiz after — architect-level, not cert-exam
  • Click module tags to go deeper on terms that need more context
🧩 Following a concept thread
  • Expand a term — read its definition and confusion note
  • Hover a Related chip to quick-peek without leaving the page
  • Click the chip to jump to that term and auto-expand it
  • Keep following related chips to trace the concept web
  • Pin terms you want to return to — they stay in the sidebar
🛡️ Incident or attack investigation
  • Filter to Identity Security domain immediately
  • Search for the attack technique (e.g. "kerberoasting", "dcsync", "aitm")
  • Read the definition — the confusion note names the exact defensive control
  • Follow Related chips to the authentication or PAM terms for remediation context
  • Use the Confusion Radar to clarify attack vs defensive term pairs
✅ Pre-interview or exam prep
  • Run Test Yourself across all 12 domains — note which ones you struggle with
  • Expand All in your weak domains and read every definition in full
  • Use the Confusion Radar — interviewers love testing conflated pairs
  • Pin the terms you keep getting wrong — study them last before the interview
  • Check the footer domain grid as a coverage checklist

1
Orient before you read terms
Every domain section has a Mind Map button. Open it first — even for 30 seconds. Seeing the spatial relationships between concepts before reading 20–34 individual definitions dramatically improves retention. Your brain builds a mental scaffold to hang each term on.
→ Mind Map first, terms second
2
Read the confusion notes — they are the most important part
Every ⚠️ note answers: "What is this term NOT, and why does that distinction matter architecturally?" In real IAM work, the knowledge gap is rarely not knowing a definition — it is conflating two closely related concepts. The confusion notes are where this dictionary earns its keep.
→ If you only read one thing per term, read the ⚠️ note
3
Use domain filtering + Expand All for a full reading session
Click a domain pill → Expand All → read top to bottom. This gives you all definitions in a domain in one sequential reading session, with no extra clicks. Collapse All when done to reset. This is the fastest way to do a full domain refresh before a design sprint or certification.
→ Domain pill → Expand All → read → Collapse All
4
Test before you study — not after
Run the Test Yourself quiz in a domain before expanding any terms. Wrong answers immediately identify your actual blind spots — and reading definitions right after a failed question locks them in with far more force than passive reading. Failure is the most efficient study signal.
→ Quiz → identify gaps → read those terms → re-quiz
5
Pin your session set before meetings
Before any architecture review, design session, or interview, spend 5 minutes pinning the 5–8 terms most likely to be relevant. They appear in the left sidebar and stay there for your whole session. You can jump to any definition in one click without breaking your attention flow mid-conversation.
→ Pin before the meeting, jump during it
6
Follow the concept web, not the domain boundaries
IAM concepts do not respect domain lines — JIT Access lives in PAM but connects to ZSP (Foundations), IRSA (Machine Identity), and Conditional Access (Cloud Identity). Use Related chips to follow threads across domains. The dictionary is a graph, not a book — the richest understanding comes from tracing connections, not reading linearly.
→ Hover chips to peek, click to traverse the graph
