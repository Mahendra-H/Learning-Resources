Real Scenario

The Report That Changed Everything

I had been CISO for two years when I commissioned our first full Red Team engagement. I told myself we were doing it because it was best practice. The truth is I was confident. We had invested heavily in our defenses. Our SOC metrics looked excellent. Our vulnerability program was mature. The Red Team spent three weeks in our environment. They walked out with: A full domain compromise. Access to our financial systems. 14,000 employee records. And a signed letter from our CFO's email account that they could have used to authorize a wire transfer. Not because our team was bad. Because they had never been tested this way. The Red Team's report was the most valuable document I received in my entire tenure as CISO. It showed me exactly where our defenses were theory and where they were reality.
A Red Team does not find failures. It finds the gap between what you believe your security posture is and what it actually is. That gap is the most dangerous thing in cybersecurity.
Team One

The Red Team.

They are on your side. They just do not act like it.

M
MENTOR
The first thing I tell every engineer who joins a security organization: a Red Team is not a threat. It is a gift. They use the exact same techniques as real attackers — OSINT, phishing, exploitation, lateral movement, privilege escalation. The difference is they hand you the receipts afterward.
L
LEARNER
So they are essentially running a real attack — but in a controlled way?
M
MENTOR
Controlled in terms of rules of engagement and scope. Within that scope, they hold nothing back. They will call your employees pretending to be IT support. They will send phishing emails. They will exploit a misconfigured server at 2am if that is what a real attacker would do. The goal is to simulate reality as closely as possible — because the only thing worse than a Red Team finding a critical gap is a real attacker finding it first.
L
LEARNER
What does their output actually look like? Is it just a list of vulnerabilities?
M
MENTOR
That is one of the most important distinctions in this space. A Red Team's output is not a vulnerability list — it is a narrative. A full attack story. Step one through step forty-seven. Here is how we got in. Here is how we moved. Here is where your monitoring failed to see us. Here is what we achieved and what the business impact would have been if we were real adversaries. That narrative is what makes executives listen in a way a CVSS score never will.
L
LEARNER
How is this different from just hiring external penetration testers?
Operation Lifecycle
Operation Classified
Operation: Glass House
Phase 01
Scoping
Define objectives, rules of engagement, and boundaries. What can the team target? What is off-limits? What constitutes mission success?
Phase 02
Recon
OSINT, network mapping, employee profiling, technology fingerprinting. Build a complete picture of the target environment before touching anything.
Phase 03
Compromise
Initial access attempt using real attacker TTPs — phishing, credential stuffing, exploitation of exposed services. Document every step.
Phase 04
Objectives
Reach defined goals: data exfiltration simulation, domain compromise, financial system access, executive email takeover. Demonstrate real business impact.
Phase 05
Report
Full findings delivered to CISO and defensive teams. Complete attack narrative, evidence, detection gaps, and prioritized remediation roadmap.
All findings are shared with defensive teams. The goal is not to win. It is to make the defenders impossible to beat.

Key Insight

Penetration testing tells you where the holes are. Red Teaming tells you whether an attacker could walk through those holes undetected, move through your environment, and achieve a real-world objective — while your entire security team is watching.

The Collaboration Model

Purple Team.

What happens when attackers and defenders learn together in the same room.

M
MENTOR
Here is something that surprises most people: Purple Team is not actually a team. It is a methodology. There is no Purple Team department. What exists is a structured exercise where Red and Blue operate together — in the same room, sharing findings in real time — so defenders can immediately improve detection while the Red Team is still testing.
L
LEARNER
So instead of Red going away for three weeks and coming back with a report, they are running exercises together?
M
MENTOR
Exactly. The Red Operator says: "I just ran this credential attack technique and your SIEM did not fire." The Blue Analyst says: "Show me exactly what that looked like in the logs." Together they build a detection rule on the spot. Red tests it again. Blue refines. Red tests again. In three hours they accomplish what would take six months in a traditional engagement cycle. The learning is immediate and bidirectional.

PURPLE TEAM
Shared learning
M
MENTOR
Most organizations run Red Team once a year and spend six months acting on the report. Purple Team changes the model entirely. The improvement cycle that used to take six months now takes an afternoon. Every technique the Red Team tries becomes a detection capability the Blue Team builds — immediately, while the attack is fresh.
Purple Team is what happens when you stop treating offense and defense as separate philosophies and start treating them as a feedback loop.
IAM Connection

The IAM team benefits enormously from Purple Team exercises. Attackers target identity systems first — always. Purple Team exercises involving credential attacks, privilege escalation, and identity-based lateral movement give IAM Architects real data on which controls work and which do not. Every Purple Team exercise should have an IAM representative in the room.

Team Two

Security Architecture.

They design systems where attackers have nowhere to enter.

M
MENTOR
Security Architects are not operators. They do not manage alerts or respond to incidents. They live at the beginning — before a single line of code is written, before a single server is provisioned. They answer one question: how should this system be designed so that security is a property of the design, not a layer added on top afterward?
L
LEARNER
So they are in every major project from day one?
M
MENTOR
Every major one. New cloud deployments. New applications. New infrastructure build-outs. Mergers and acquisitions — which are a security nightmare without proper architecture review. New vendor integrations. Any time an organization is making a significant technology decision, a Security Architect should be in the room before the decision is made, not called in afterward to patch what was built wrong.
Architecture vs Engineering
🗺️
Security Architecture
"What should we build?"

Security Architects define the shape of secure systems before they exist. They produce threat models, reference architectures, and security standards that engineering teams implement. Their work happens at the intersection of business requirements, risk tolerance, and technical possibility — translating organizational risk appetite into concrete design decisions. They specify authentication patterns, network segmentation strategies, data classification controls, and cryptographic requirements.

🔧
Security Engineering
"How do we build it?"

Security Engineers take architectural specifications and turn them into working systems. They configure, integrate, automate, and deploy the security controls that Architects design. They build SIEM integrations, implement PAM vaults, engineer IAM platforms, and deploy cloud security controls. Their work is measured not in documents but in running systems. They bridge the gap between security strategy and operational reality.

Architecture designs → Engineering implements → Both verify with Red Team
L
LEARNER
This is actually really clear when you frame it as "what to build" vs "how to build it." In engineering we call that design vs implementation.
M
MENTOR
Exactly the right parallel. And just like in engineering, the best outcomes happen when Architects and Engineers work together from the start — not when Architects throw specifications over the wall. The worst security implementations I have seen were built by excellent Engineers who received a bad Architecture. The best ones were built by Engineers who were part of the Architecture process from day one.

Team Three

Security Engineering.

They build what Architecture designs.

M
MENTOR
Security Engineering is where security controls become real. The architect designs a Zero Trust network — the security engineer builds it. The architect specifies PAM controls — the security engineer implements the vault and the session recording. Every security capability that runs in production exists because a Security Engineer built it.
L
LEARNER
How is this different from the IT team that just maintains systems day to day?
M
MENTOR
That is the critical distinction. Security Engineers are builders, not maintainers. They deliver new capabilities and then hand them off to operations teams who run them. A Security Engineer who spends their career patching the same firewall is in the wrong role. Their job is to create the next layer of defense — SIEM integrations, IAM platforms, automation pipelines, cloud security controls — and then move on to the next build. The work is always net-new.
IAM Connection

For IAM Architects, Security Engineering is the most important operational partner. When you design an identity governance program, Security Engineers build the platform it runs on. When you specify PAM controls, Security Engineers implement the vault and the session recording. Understanding what Security Engineers need — clear requirements, testable specifications, and realistic timelines — makes IAM programs dramatically more successful.

The Cultural Shift

DevSecOps.

Security moves left. Into the pipeline. Into the code. Before it ships.

M
MENTOR
DevSecOps is not a team you hire. It is a philosophy — and increasingly, a set of tools and practices that live inside engineering pipelines. The traditional model: engineers build, security reviews after the fact, security finds problems, engineers fix them, repeat. Slow, expensive, and adversarial. Everyone resents it.
L
LEARNER
I have lived that cycle. Security review gates that block releases for weeks because nobody caught the issue at design time.
M
MENTOR
Exactly. DevSecOps eliminates that gate by making security a property of the development process itself. Static analysis in the IDE before a developer commits. Dependency scanning that catches vulnerable libraries before they ship. Container security scanning. Infrastructure-as-code review that flags misconfigurations before they reach production. All automated. All in the pipeline. The developer gets feedback in seconds, not weeks.

Red Team

Security Architecture

DevSecOps
Secrets management, service identities, pipeline authorization, and deployment audit trails are all identity problems. IAM Architects who understand DevSecOps pipelines design the identity layer for modern engineering organizations — and are in extremely high demand as a result.
