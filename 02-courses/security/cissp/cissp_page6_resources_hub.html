📊 When to Start Practice Questions

Start domain-specific questions from Week 1 as you study each domain. Begin full mixed-domain sets in Phase 3 (final 6 weeks). Target 75%+ on Boson consistently before booking the real exam.

🗓️ Registration Guide

How to Register for the Exam

Step-by-step from zero to booked. No confusion, no missed steps. Follow this exact sequence.

1
Create Your (ISC)² Account
Visit isc2.org and create a free candidate account. This account will track your exam registration, eventual certification, and CPE credits throughout your career. Use your full legal name exactly as it appears on your government-issued ID — this name must match on exam day.
→ isc2.org/register
2
Confirm Your Work Experience Qualifies
You need 5 cumulative years of paid, full-time work experience in 2 or more of the 8 CISSP domains. Your 18 years of IT experience covering Domain 5 (IAM/SSO) and Domain 8 (Software Security) qualifies you already. Have your employment history ready — you will need to document this during the endorsement process after passing.
→ Your Domain 5 + Domain 8 experience = full qualification
3
Schedule via Pearson VUE
From your (ISC)² candidate portal, select "Schedule Examination" → CISSP. You will be redirected to Pearson VUE's scheduling system. Choose between a physical test centre location near you or an online proctored exam taken from your home or office. Browse available dates and times — popular centres book 2-4 weeks in advance so plan accordingly.
→ pearsonvue.com/isc2
4
Pay the Exam Fee
The CISSP exam fee is $749 USD. Payment is required at the time of booking through Pearson VUE. The fee is non-refundable but the exam can be rescheduled with at least 48 hours notice. Some employers in tech and financial services will reimburse this cost — check with your HR team before paying personally.
→ $749 USD · Non-refundable · Reschedule possible with 48hr notice
5
Prepare Your ID for Exam Day
Bring two forms of identification. Primary: a government-issued photo ID with your signature (passport, national ID, or driver's licence). The name on your ID must exactly match the name on your (ISC)² registration. Secondary: any additional ID with your name. No electronic devices, study materials, or personal items are permitted in the test room.
→ Primary ID must have photo + signature + name matching registration
6
Sit the CAT Exam (125-175 Questions, 3 Hours)
Arrive 30-45 minutes early for check-in procedures. You will receive scratch paper and a marker — immediately write your key frameworks (PICERL, CIA Triad, STRIDE, ALE formula). The exam adapts to your performance. A provisional pass or fail result is shown immediately on screen when the exam ends. The official confirmation letter arrives within 3-5 business days.
→ Provisional result shown immediately on screen after completing the exam
7
Endorsement — Get Sponsored by an (ISC)² Member
After passing, you have 9 months to complete the endorsement process. You need a current (ISC)² certified professional to sponsor your application by confirming your professional experience. If you do not know anyone with an (ISC)² certification, (ISC)² itself can endorse you directly — contact them at endorsement@isc2.org and they will facilitate the process. After endorsement approval, pay the Annual Maintenance Fee ($125/year) and you are officially CISSP certified.
→ 9 months to complete endorsement · Annual Maintenance Fee: $125/year → You Are CISSP Certified! 🏆
🔄 CPE Maintenance

Keeping Your CISSP Active

CISSP is not a one-and-done credential. It requires 120 CPE (Continuing Professional Education) credits over every 3-year cycle plus the $125 Annual Maintenance Fee. Here is how to earn those credits easily.

📖
Reading Security Content
Reading security books, white papers, research papers, and professional articles counts toward CPE credits. Track your reading using (ISC)² CPE Portal.
Up to 40 credits/year · Group A
🎓
Online Courses and Training
Completing online security courses (Udemy, Coursera, LinkedIn Learning, vendor training) earns 1 CPE credit per hour of instruction. Take courses in areas where you want to grow.
1 CPE per training hour · Group A
🎤
Attending Conferences and Events
Security conferences like DEF CON, Black Hat, RSA Conference, BSides events, and (ISC)² Security Congress all qualify. Virtual attendance also counts for CPE credits.
1 CPE per hour attended · Group A
✍️
Writing and Publishing
Writing blog posts, technical articles, white papers, or books about security topics earns CPE credits. As a Java developer writing about secure coding or IAM, you have natural content to produce.
Up to 40 credits/year · Group A
🏢
Professional Experience
Simply doing your job in a security-related role earns 10 CPE credits per year under Group B. Your ongoing work in IAM development and security engineering counts automatically.
10 credits/year automatically · Group B
👥
Volunteering and Mentoring
Volunteering for (ISC)², mentoring other security professionals, participating in (ISC)² chapter events, or contributing to security community efforts all earn CPE credits. Giving back earns forward.
Up to 10 credits/year · Group B

💡 CPE Made Easy: Your ongoing Java development and IAM work earns you 10 free credits per year (Group B). Taking one 10-hour Udemy course per year earns another 10. Reading one security book per quarter earns approximately 15-20 more. Attending one conference per year earns 8-16 credits. You can easily hit 40+ credits per year without disrupting your normal professional activities.
